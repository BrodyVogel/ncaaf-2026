## 2. CONFERENCE-WIDE NEWS

**Commissioner:** Tony Petitti VERIFIED as sitting Commissioner; opened media days Tuesday, July 28. (bigten.org; Hoosier Huddle, Jul 28, 2026)

- [ALL] [QUOTE] Petitti on 24-team playoff eliminating the Big Ten title game: "Yeah, I think it's pretty much we won't be playing a championship game if we go to 24… It's still very powerful to win a Big Ten championship, and teams play hard." (Source: Hoosier State of Mind, Jul 28, 2026; AP via kens5.com, Jul 2026)
- [ALL] [REPORTED] Petitti reiterated his pitch for a 24-team CFP, citing "keeping as many teams in contention for as long as possible." The Big Ten was first Power Four league to back 24 teams; Big 12, ACC and Notre Dame followed; SEC/Greg Sankey opposed. (Source: On3, Jul 2026; AP via kens5.com)
- [ALL] [QUOTE] Petitti pushing back on SEC depth claims: "The idea that the middle of our conference isn't hard? That's not right." (Source: CBS Sports, Jul 28, 2026)
- [ALL] [QUOTE] Petitti on Big Ten–SEC rivalry games: "Our fans, and I think the SEC fans, they really like the rivalry." (Source: 247Sports, Jul 2026)
- [ALL] [QUOTE] Petitti on non-conference scheduling / playoff clarity: "We've talked a lot about scheduling in our AD room and what's coming next… Our focus now is seeing clarity around the Playoff, and access and how that's going to work before we make the next decision." (Source: FootballScoop, Jul 28, 2026)
- [ALL] [REPORTED] The Big Ten remains the only major conference not requiring a Power Four non-conference game annually; Petitti said scheduling decisions await a CFP-format decision. (Source: AP via ClickOnDetroit, Jul 30, 2026)
- [ALL] [QUOTE] Petitti on legislation/House settlement language: "That's all very specific language that has to work together with the (House) settlement, so we need to see the language on all of that." (Source: AP via ClickOnDetroit, Jul 28, 2026)
- [ALL] [REPORTED] House settlement terms: schools may share **up to a 22% cap of average revenue, set at roughly $20.5M per school for 2025-26**, projected to rise to an estimated $32.9M by 2034-35. (Source: CBS Sports, Jun 6, 2025 — PRE-baseline context; corroborated by Buchanan Ingersoll & Rooney / Loeb & Loeb analyses)
- [ALL] [REPORTED] The bill is the **Protect College Sports Act of 2026 (S.4668, 119th Congress)**. Revised language "creates a retention pool exception of $20 million above the existing revenue-share cap, with up to an additional $5 million available exclusively for women's institutional NIL"; the Big Ten/SEC had originally requested $25M. Sen. Eric Schmitt cited a $22.5M retention pool atop the $21.3M cap for a "$48.8 million total" athlete-pay ceiling. (Source: CBS Sports, Jul 2026; Yahoo Sports, Jul 2026)
- [ALL] [QUOTE] Sen. Ted Cruz (@SenTedCruz, Jul 30, 2026), on the retention exception not being a loophole: "The cap is a hard cap. Full stop. That loophole is closed."
- [ALL] [REPORTED] Big Ten and SEC endorsed the bill via joint statement after negotiations with Senators Cruz, Cantwell and Schmitt; Senate future uncertain before the August recess. (Source: CBS Sports, Jul 2026; SI, Jul 2026; CNN, Jul 28, 2026)
- [NEBRASKA] [REPORTED] The College Sports Commission approved $7.5M in restructured NIL deals for 18 Nebraska football players after initial rejection (upheld by an arbitrator two months earlier); AD Troy Dannen announced it at media days. (Source: AP via kens5.com, Jul 2026 — *note: an authoritative primary source with the exact $7.5M/18-player figure was not independently located; retained per AP*)
- [ALL] [REPORTED] Context: SEC presidents/chancellors approved a move to a **nine-game conference schedule beginning 2026** (announced Aug. 21, 2025), matching the Big Ten and Big 12; format is 3 annual + 6 rotating opponents. Commissioner Greg Sankey: "Adding a ninth SEC game underscores our universities' commitment to delivering the most competitive football schedule in the nation." (Source: ESPN/FOX Sports/SEC/AP, Aug. 21, 2025 — PRE-baseline context)

**Coverage note:** Conference-wide business coverage is deep and consistent, dominated by CFP expansion (24-team) and federal legislation.
