## 1. Executive summary

The engine is sound. The plumbing is not.

I found no mathematical defect in `final_pass.py` or `win_engine.py` — I read both line by line and stress-tested the win engine against a conservation identity it passes to within 0.09%. The probit model, the shared-shock Gauss-Hermite integration, the alternating-projection demean/strip fix from the D1–D5 round, the un-shrink and recenter steps: all correct, all in the right order.

What is wrong lives one layer out, in the code that *feeds* the engine and the code that *reads* it. Specifically: name-space mismatches that silently drop teams from joins, a hardcoded constant that overrides the model's own FCS ratings, market prices that are synthesized rather than observed, and a sizing rule that ignores edge magnitude. Three of these touch live money tonight.

Underneath all of it sits one strategic finding that I consider more important than any individual bug, and which I develop in §3: **most of the model's disagreement with the market is generated by the grade layer, not inherited from SP+ — and the grade layer has never been backtested.** F1's 77–79% five-season validation does not cover it.

### F-4 — Every under price in your book is fabricated

`data/win_totals/win_totals_2026.csv` has five columns: `team, market, book, line, over_odds`. **There is no under price anywhere in the data.** 785 rows, zero observed under quotes.

Every under price the screen shows is synthesized by `under_odds()` from a flat 30-cent assumption. **8 of your 19 positions are unders, carrying 5.45u of 14.17u — 38% of the book.** The prices those positions were evaluated against:

Florida U7.5 → +145 · West Virginia U5.5 → +137 · Pittsburgh U7.5 → +137 · Arizona State U6.5 → +110 · Illinois U6.5 → +118 · Wisconsin U6.5 → +102 · Liberty U8.5 → −125 · Hawai'i U7.5 → −120

None of these numbers was ever observed at a book. Win totals routinely run 20–40 cents depending on book and side, and the juice is frequently asymmetric — books shade the popular side. If the true hold on a given under is 40 cents rather than 30, the displayed edge is overstated by roughly 4–5 percentage points, which is larger than the entire claimed edge on Hawai'i (+4.2% min-lens) and comparable to Illinois (+7.6%) and Wisconsin (+7.2%).

This is the cheapest high-value fix in the audit: capture actual under prices and the whole under sleeve gets honestly priced.

### F-5 — Sizing ignores edge

Section D's sizing rule (line 186) is a function of `gap` and factor tags only:

```python
size = 0.25 if (pri and nudge) else (0.25 if pri else (0.20 if nudge else 0.15))
```

`pri` is `gap >= 1.0`. Edge enters only as a gate (`ec >= 0.025`, on the *calibrated* lens), never as a magnitude, and never on the conservative min-lens. The live board shows what that produces:

- **Maryland O4.5 −172 → 0.25u PRIORITY**, min-lens edge **+0.9%**
- **Florida State O5.5 −150 → 0.25u PRIORITY**, min-lens edge **+1.3%**
- **Georgia Southern O4.5 −150 → 0.25u PRIORITY**, min-lens edge **+1.9%**
- **Vanderbilt O5.5 −130 → 0.15u**, min-lens edge **+9.6%**

Vanderbilt has 5–10× the conservative edge of the three positions being sized above it. A gap-only ladder is defensible if you believe `gap` is the real signal and edge is noise — but then the min-lens screen is theater. If you believe the edge estimate, sizing should respond to it. Right now the two rules contradict each other, and the contradiction is resolving in favor of positions with essentially no measured edge.

### F-6 — F5 is deployed in a form that was never validated

S18-A validated F5 as a **continuous** Q term: `ols([SP, RP_, NH, G5, Q], Y)` with t = **+2.83**, clearing the registered |t| ≥ 2 bar. The screen deploys it as a **binary top-quartile dummy** (line 146: `r['Q'] >= q_top`). The dummy form tests at t ≈ 1.83–1.85 — below the bar you registered.

F5 currently tags Oregon State, Bowling Green, Wake Forest, UCF, Rutgers, Duke and Kennesaw State on your board, and functions as a sizing nudge. It is doing real work in a form that did not pass its own registration.

## 3. The finding I care most about — where the edge actually comes from

This is my own diagnostic; no agent ran it. The question: when this model disagrees with the market, is that disagreement inherited from SP+ (which F1 validated at 77–79% over five seasons), or manufactured by the grade layer on top?

Working in probability units at each team's modal posted line, n = 137:

```
ANCHOR (SP+ blend) vs de-vigged market : mean +0.0101   SD 0.0633
OUR MODEL          vs de-vigged market : mean +0.0092   SD 0.0940
OUR ADDITION (model − anchor)          : mean −0.0008   SD 0.0785

corr(anchor-disagreement, our-addition)         = −0.135
variance share INHERITED from SP+               =  0.45
variance share CONTRIBUTED by our grade layer   =  0.70
```

(Shares exceed 1 because the correlation is negative — our layer partly cancels the anchor's view.)

The last number is the one that should give you pause: **our addition points the same direction as the anchor's disagreement in 67 of 137 teams — 49%. A coin flip.**

So the grade layer is not amplifying SP+'s view of where the market is wrong. It is producing a nearly orthogonal, slightly opposing view, and it accounts for the majority of the variance in the final disagreement. When the screen fires on `gap ≥ 1.0`, most of what it is firing on has never been tested against a market.

I am not saying the grade layer is wrong. I am saying its out-of-sample performance is unknown, and that the five-season validation you are implicitly leaning on measures something else. F1 backtested *consensus-vs-market*. You are betting *consensus-plus-our-grades-vs-market*, and the "plus" is the bigger term.

There is a way to find out. The grade layer's contribution is a clean per-team vector; it could be backtested against 2021–2025 closes exactly the way F1 was, provided historical grades can be reconstructed. That is the single highest-value piece of research left in this project, and it is worth more than any bug on this list.

### A related note on how edges are being displayed

The screen reports `p − breakeven`, and labels it EV. True EV per dollar is `(p − b)/b`, roughly **1.9× larger** at these prices. The board's top claims in true-EV terms: Navy U7.5 **+49.3%**, Colorado State O3.5 **+35.9%**, Nevada O4.5 **+35.4%**, Tulsa O5.5 **+32.0%**, Texas U9.5 **+30.6%**, UConn O5.5 **+28.3%**. Board-wide, 93 of 274 quotes (34%) claim positive edge; 24 claim ≥8% displayed.

A model claiming a +49% edge against a liquid, well-traded market is making an extraordinary claim. Combined with §3, the honest reading is that the *displayed* edge is a lower bound on how much of it is model error rather than market error. This does not mean the positions are bad — it means the edge estimates should not be taken at face value for sizing, which is a second argument for the F-5 fix.

## 4. Research-integrity findings

These do not change tonight's slate but they degrade confidence in the factor set.

**Survivorship contradicts the preregistration.** The S17/S18 curve panel requires a player to appear in FBS the following year with ≥6 games (`if gc < 6 or nm in FBS_Y[y] or nm not in FBS_Y[y+1]: continue`), censoring roughly 30% of intake on the outcome side. `PREREGISTRATION_S18_2026-08-02.md` states verbatim: "No survivorship — rostered intent, not realized snaps." The bias is largest for high-N teams — Toledo (N=21) is the F5 archetype.

**Q's effect is tail-concentrated and fragile.** The top 6 of 528 rows carry 49.4% of the Q–miss covariance. ΔR² = 0.0134. In a joint model with N, Q's coefficient flips negative (t = −0.16).

**Independent-denominator artifact in S18-B and S13.** `zone['c'][1]` and `zone['k'][1]` each increment on their own arm's condition, so the headline "71.4% → 72.4%" compares two different bet sets. On the like-for-like common subset the agent reports 15/21 = 71.4% both ways with zero side-call flips (S13: 19/24 = 79.2% both ways, zero flips). I have confirmed the code pattern directly; I have not independently re-derived the counts.

**Multiplicity.** Roughly 120 statistical looks with zero corrections, five acceptance decisions resting on the same ~28 bets, and 20 of 20 registration→findings pairs committed within 13 minutes of each other. Preregistration that lands 13 minutes before its result is not preregistration.

**`b1_fit.py` neutral-site double-count and sign error** (in-season path only, not the win-total board): ~45% of neutral games are emitted twice, and the duplicate's `act` is away-oriented while `-ln[1]` is home-oriented. Correcting orientation moves market MAE by +0.085/+0.101/+0.102 — the published "+0.050, within a nickel of the close" becomes roughly +0.15.
