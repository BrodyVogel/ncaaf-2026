## Question

Do the market's (SP+) **preseason→final** conference-level errors persist year over year? A persistent conference error would be an exploitable market bias; noise corroborates demeaning the residual by conference. Per owner policy, even a positive result does not reverse the demean decision (realignment non-stationarity).

## Method

- error(team, year) = final SP+ overall (CFBD `/ratings/sp`) − preseason SP+ overall (uploaded). Same scale (pts vs FBS average).

- Conference = that season's **as-played** membership from CFBD `/teams/fbs?year=` (the `/ratings/sp` conference field is forward-looking — it labels realigning G5 teams by their 2026 destination — and is NOT used). Conf-mean error kept when ≥3 matched teams.

- Pearson r of conf-mean errors across adjacent years (conferences present in both). Split: pre-realignment pairs (2021→22, 2022→23) pooled; 2023→24 straddles realignment (flagged); 2024→25 post-realignment.

## Confound check: preseason-dispersion compression

If SP+ preseason is more dispersed than final (a known property), high-rated teams drift DOWN and low-rated teams drift UP by construction. Because conferences are level clusters (P4 high, G5 low), that compression alone manufactures a P4-negative / G5-positive error pattern with **no** conference edge behind it.

- Pooled all team-years (n=664): corr(preseason rating, error) = **-0.32**, slope(error ~ preseason) = **-0.196**. Negative — high-rated teams systematically finish below their preseason number. This is the same scale-compression confound cited in the decision record (HANDOFF §6).

## Reading

- **Pre-realignment persistence is null** (pooled r=-0.27, |r|<0.3, n=22): idiosyncratic conference-level preseason errors do NOT repeat year to year — consistent with noise, corroborating the demean decision.
- **The post-realignment pair (2024→25) shows r=+0.69** — reported honestly, not buried. But it is a SINGLE year-transition (one correlation over ~10 conferences), and the conference-mean vector it correlates is dominated by the P4/G5 level axis, which the compression confound above already explains mechanically (G5 over, P4 under, every year). It is the P4/G5 ordering re-expressing itself, not evidence of a per-conference edge. Under the owner's standard ('significant evidence' for a class edge; per-conference edges distrusted) one noisy post-realignment pair, largely attributable to compression, does not clear the bar.
- Why demeaning is unaffected either way: the demean acts on the grade→points **residual**, not on the anchor. Whatever real conference/level information the market carries is already in the anchor blend and flows through to every final rating untouched; demeaning only neutralizes conference-correlated structure in the grade-adjustment layer. So even a real market conference bias would not argue against demeaning the residual.
- The 2023→24 straddle pair mixes old/new membership and is reported for completeness only. Small n (≤11 conferences/year, 5 seasons) caps confidence regardless of sign — this is corroboration, not a test the grades could ever pass or fail directly (no historical grades exist).

## Bottom line

This is anchor-side (SP+) evidence, the closest available proxy since the grades themselves have no history. It informs confidence in the demean decision but, by the owner's stated standard, cannot by itself change it. Decision unchanged: conference-demeaned residual remains OFFICIAL.
