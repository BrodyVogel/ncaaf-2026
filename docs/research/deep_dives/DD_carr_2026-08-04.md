## Step 6 — Adversarial pass: the OVER case

Notre Dame enters 2026 as a consensus top-4 team whose one offseason
subtraction is the thing that suppressed Carr's volume. For two years the
identity was "hand it to two future first-rounders behind an elite line" —
that option is gone, and in its place are five legitimate receivers,
including two Ohio State five-stars who left Columbus specifically to
play. The scout's line — they asked little of him and he was very good
anyway — is the scariest sentence in our own files: 9.2 yards an attempt,
number-3 in the country, 24-6 TD-INT, a 66% completion rate, on a bare 25.7
attempts a game. The line requires 26. Every marginal attempt the RB
exodus forces is nearly a full expected point of yardage over 2875.5, and
Freeman's staff knows a Heisman-track QB season is worth more to the
program than a fourth rushing title. Availability won't save the under
here the way it does elsewhere: he takes eight sacks a year, never
scrambles, and plays behind the country's #12 pass-pro line — P(12+) ≈
0.55 is the highest on the board, and given twelve games the under is
already a 43/57 dog at the fit, worse at the cuffs-off μ. This leg is the
board's purest bet that a program CHOOSES to keep the cuffs on.
