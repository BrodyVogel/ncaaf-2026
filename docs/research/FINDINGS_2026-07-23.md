## The headline: our flat conference offsets are a *bad forecaster* — worse than doing nothing

Predicting a mover's next-year grade with the production offsets
(`grade + offset_origin − offset_dest`) yields MAE **9.93**, worse than naively carrying
the grade forward (**8.92**); the fitted model gets **7.15**. Why: the offsets were fitted
to translate as-played grades onto a common SP+-anchored *scale* — a legitimate purpose —
but as a *forecast* they double-count the level gap and ignore the single biggest fact in
the data: **grade_t transfers to grade_t+1 with slope ≈0.40**. The correct transfer
forecast is mostly *shrinkage toward the mean*, plus small class-jump terms:

- **P4→G5: +1.45 pts** vs stayers (se 0.48) — real but modest, nothing like the +5–14
  the offsets imply. By prior usage: low-usage movers gain least (+3.97 raw), mid
  (+5.97) and high (+5.41) most, vs stayers' +1.69 — reliability of the prior tape, not
  hidden upside, drives the translation.
- **G5→P4: −3.54 pts** vs stayers (se 0.32) — the step-up discount is real and roughly
  *half* what the flat offsets charge in typical cases.
- Lateral moves: **zero** net effect.
- Destination-conference residuals are small (±1.6, report-only): movers to the MAC/
  independents mildly overperform the model, to the Big 12/Pac-12 mildly underperform.

**Nuance that keeps this honest:** the offsets are not "wrong" for their original job
(scaling a season that already happened). The error is using scale-translation as
performance-projection — which the grading process does implicitly whenever it maps a
transfer's origin-conference tape into the new context.

## Transfer ratings: the owner was right

With prior tape and volume known, portal stars add ~0.9% R² (misses the 1% bar). Worse,
for entrants with **no prior FBS tape** — exactly where a rating would be most useful —
stars correlate **0.07** with the first FBS-season grade. Transfer ratings are
recycled recruiting pedigree plus name recognition; they are not a usable prior. Do not
add them to the grading manual.

## The shrinkage table (Study 2's deliverable)

w(n) = n/(n+k): the weight a grade on n units of volume deserves, remainder to the
position mean. Fold-stable k:

| Group | k (volume units) | A full starter season earns ≈ |
|---|---|---|
| QB | **230** dropbacks | w ≈ 0.60 (~350 db) |
| RB | **110** carries | w ≈ 0.62 (~180 att) |
| WR/TE | **190** routes | w ≈ 0.65 (~350 rt) |
| OL | **595** snaps | w ≈ 0.57 (~800 sn) |
| DL | **290** snaps | w ≈ 0.63 (~500 sn) |
| LB | **630** snaps | w ≈ 0.52 (~700 sn) |
| DB | **1180** snaps | w ≈ 0.40 (~800 sn) |

Read the last row twice: **a full season of cornerback tape deserves 40% weight.** DB (and
LB) grades are the least reliable objects in the sport — which retroactively validates
every "LB caution rule" instinct in the dossiers and says our confident small-sample DB
reads were never justified. Caveat (S2-A): QB and LB reliability dips at the very top
volume bucket (starters entering year-t+1 system changes, plausibly); practical impact is
small since w caps ~0.5–0.6 there anyway, but cap w at the second-highest bucket's value
for those two groups if adopted.

Aging curves: everyone improves ~+1.3 to +1.9 among stayers with no clean class gradient,
and FR/SO year-effects flip sign across seasons → **fails the pre-registered sign-stability
condition; not shipped.**

## Recommended production changes (awaiting owner sign-off)

1. **Adopt the translation forecast for transfer grading** (replaces flat offsets when
   projecting a transfer's next-year performance): shrink via w(n), then apply the
   class-jump term. Scope options: (a) future grading only, or (b) plus a **targeted
   audit of 2026 grades on transfer-heavy units** — the model says our G5→P4 discounts
   are probably too harsh and our P4→G5 boosts too generous, which is a *directional,
   testable* disagreement with the current board.
2. **Adopt the w(n)=n/(n+k) table** as the standard small-sample anchor (qualitative
   deviations still allowed, but now they're explicit acts against a number).
3. **Reject portal stars** as a grading input (confirmed null).
4. Aging term: not shipped (failed stability).

## Limitations
FBS-only panel (FCS→FBS entrants enter via priors, not translation); reliability
conflates measurement noise with true year-over-year change (acceptable: the production
question is the composite); 4 transitions; portal name-matching 89%; ~1% of rows dropped
on unresolvable team abbreviations; volume floors disclosed above (chosen once, not
tuned). Study 3 (continuity × prior-stability) deferred to next session per plan.

# Addendum — Study 2b: HS composites as the small-sample prior (run 2026-07-23, after registration)

Data: CFBD HS recruiting classes 2017–2025 (27,186 rated recruits), matched to 9,284 of
14,292 first-meaningful-volume panel players (65%). Code: the S2b block registered in
the pre-registration; pull cached at `data/cfbd/recruiting_players/`.

| Bar | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| S2b-A pooled partial ≥ 0.15 | **0.073** (n=9,284) | **FAIL** |
| S2b-B late-breakout slice | 0.052 (n=7,826) — positive but tiny | Moot given A; the owner's motivating case is where the signal is DEADEST |
| Registered fallback: true-freshman-only | **0.266** (n=1,458) — nearly 2× the bar | **SHIPS, freshman-scope only** |

**The story the data tells:** HS pedigree is real information about a player *until college
reveals better information.* For true freshmen thrown straight onto the field, the
composite predicts first tape strongly (partial 0.266; 5★ 68.1 vs 3★ 61.9 — a 6-point
gap; slope **+4.7 grade pts per 0.10 of composite**). But for the player who waits 2–3
years for his first real snaps — the owner's 5★-in-year-3 case — the signal collapses to
0.05: **being made to wait IS the update.** Coaches watch practice every day; the
playing-time decision has already priced the pedigree, and a buried 5★ is no longer a 5★.
(Related survivorship wrinkle: 2★s who eventually reach meaningful volume out-grade 3★s —
selection, not talent.)

**Shipped production rule (awaiting owner sign-off, freshman scope only):** for a true
freshman projected to play with no college tape, prior = position mean +
47 × (composite − 0.861); w(n) shrinks observed tape toward that prior as snaps arrive.
For year-2+ first-timers, the prior stays the position mean — pedigree gets no weight,
per the data. QB is the strongest per-group signal (0.14 pooled), LB is zero.

Comparison for the record: transfer-portal stars failed at 0.072–0.093 everywhere;
HS composites fail in general but pass decisively in the one scope where no better
information exists yet. The unifying principle across S1-C/S1-D/S2b: **rankings only
carry signal where tape does not yet exist — and the moment tape (or a coach's
playing-time verdict) exists, it dominates.**

# Addendum 2 — S1-E (origin talent) and S2-C (career pooling): both FAIL their bars

**S1-E — the "Georgia backup → UAB" hypothesis: NULL.** Origin-team talent (z-scored
composite) adds ΔR² **0.0001** on thin-tape movers (bar 0.01), coefficient **+0.13 grade
pts per SD** of origin talent — sign-stable across all folds but trivially small (all
movers: ΔR² 0.0014, +0.52/SD). Roster brand does not travel: a backup at Georgia was
made a backup BY Georgia's coaches, and that verdict is the information. The validated
+1.45 P4→G5 term already contains everything the origin class carries. **Not shipped.**
This completes a four-for-four sweep: portal stars (S1-C/D), HS composites past year one
(S2b), and origin-roster brand (S1-E) all die the moment any on-field or playing-time
information exists.

**S2-C — career pooling: right direction, under the bar. Not shipped.** Recency-decayed
career evidence beats single-season by **+0.1%** overall (bar 1%), **+0.3%** multi-history
(bar 2%), **+0.5%** movers, **+0.8%** on the journeyman slice (multi-history + thin
current tape, n=1,421) — never worse anywhere, helping most exactly where theory says,
but far below the registered thresholds. Why so small: w(n) shrinkage already absorbs
most sample noise, and decay 0.5 makes last season dominate for typical players. Verdict
per pre-registration: fails; single-season evidence remains the formula input. Logged as
a 2027-build candidate with decay as a fitted (not fixed) parameter — the one variant
this round was barred from trying.

**Effect on the v2 spec: none.** The pro forma proceeds with exactly the previously
validated components: w(n) shrinkage + class-jump terms + freshman composite priors.
Owner's two cases resolve as: Georgia-backup boost = the +1.45 term plus (stingy,
logged) qualitative deviation; journeyman pinning = w(n) on his current-season volume
toward the position mean, with pooling available informally to the grader but not in
the formula.

# Addendum 3 — S2-D: the comeback case (Penix question)

**S2-D-A — missed a FULL year: PASSES (+4.3%, bar 2%).** n=340 gap pairs (in panel at t
and t+2, absent t+1). Decayed old tape beats a prior-only forecast of the return season
decisively; the gap slope is **0.279** (vs 0.396 for one-year-old tape — two-year-old
tape keeps ~70% of fresh tape's predictive weight), sign-stable across folds.
**SHIPS as the look-through rule:** for a player returning from a fully missed season,
evidence = his last graded season with volume halved (one extra decay), then standard
w(n). A Penix-type (80-grade on 400 db, one year lost) projects ~74.5, not the 69.7
position mean the formula gave him before this rule.

**S2-D-B — hampered season (vol < 25% of prior year, same team): FAILS, informatively.**
n=429. Look-through to the pre-injury season is WORSE (−1.9%) than simply using the tiny
hampered-season tape; a 50/50 blend is neutral; comeback intercept −0.96 (no
under-prediction to correct). Explanation that makes this coherent: PFF grades are
per-snap rate quality — a hampered player's limited snaps still measure him honestly
(hampered-season mean grade 62.3 ≈ pre-injury 62.3), so the small sample is unbiased and
w(n) already weights it correctly. **No change shipped; v2-single already handles
played-hurt cases.**

Scope note: only single-year gaps were testable (panel = 5 seasons); multi-year gaps
default to prior-only until more data. Cause (injury/redshirt/suspension) is
unobservable and irrelevant to the projection math, per registration.
